I am a Ph.D. candidate in the Department of Physics and Astronomy at Tufts University, working in the group of Prof. Timothy J. Atherton. My research sits at the intersection of computational physics, soft matter and optimization: I build finite-element models of liquid crystals and use them to understand how order, defects and interfaces shape the behavior of soft materials. Before Tufts, I received my B.S. and M.S. in Physics from the Universidad Autónoma Metropolitana, Iztapalapa, in Mexico City.

Research interests
======
I am interested in liquid crystals, materials that flow like liquids but keep some of the order of a crystal. They come in many flavors, and I study them from the computational side. My current work covers:

* **Ferroelectric nematics**: polar liquid crystals, which I simulate using finite-element tools within [Morpho](https://github.com/Morpho-lang/morpho).
* **Liquid crystal nanocomposites**: nematics mixed with nanoparticles, where I model the formation of tactoids and nanoparticle condensates.
* **Bee swarms**: collective systems that can be described with the same Q-tensor theory used for liquid crystals.

Besides Morpho, I also run simulations with FEniCS and HOOMD-blue.
